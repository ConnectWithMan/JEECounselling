What is JEE Main Cutoff 2027?

The JEE Main cutoff is the minimum NTA Score (percentile) that a candidate must secure to qualify for JEE Advanced and become eligible for IIT admissions. NTA announces separate cutoffs for each category — General, EWS, OBC-NCL, SC, ST, and PwD. Separately, NIT/IIIT/GFTI closing ranks (CRL) released by JoSAA after seat allotment represent the admission cutoff for those institutes.

Factors Affecting JEE Main Cutoff 2027

Number of Aspirants

More candidates means higher competition. A 13 lakh+ candidate pool means every percentile point is contested by thousands of students, pushing the cutoff higher.

Paper Difficulty Level

If the exam is difficult, fewer candidates score high — lowering the absolute cutoff. If it's easier, more score high — raising the cutoff. NTA normalizes via percentile.

JEE Advanced Seat Count

NTA selects top 2,50,000 unique candidates. Category-wise allocation percentages directly determine how many candidates clear each category cutoff.

Percentile Normalization

JEE Main is held in multiple sessions with different papers. NTA uses percentile normalization to equate scores across shifts. Your relative position in the session matters most.

How to Use JEE Main Cutoff to Plan Admission

Step-by-Step Approach

  • Check your NTA Score from your JEE Main scorecard.
  • Compare with category-wise qualifying cutoff to confirm JEE Advanced eligibility.
  • Note your CRL rank and category rank from NTA's rank list.
  • Use previous year JoSAA closing ranks to shortlist target colleges.
  • Build a choice-filling list with safe, moderate, and reach options.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Don't compare CRL rank with category-wise closing ranks — they are different metrics.
  • Don't ignore HS vs OS quota — HS gives a significant rank advantage at your home state NIT.
  • Don't rely on Round 1 closing ranks — Round 6 (final) ranks are more realistic.
  • Don't ignore gender-neutral vs female-only seat closing ranks — they differ significantly.
