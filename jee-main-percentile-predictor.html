How NTA Calculates the Percentile Score — Official Methodology

The National Testing Agency (NTA) computes the NTA Score (Percentile) using a normalization process that accounts for varying difficulty across different exam shifts. Candidates from tougher shifts are not disadvantaged compared to those in easier ones.

Official NTA Percentile Formula:

NTA Percentile = [ 100 x (No. of candidates in session scoring ≤ Raw Score) ] ÷ (Total candidates in that session)

CRL Rank Estimation Formula:

CRL Rank ≈ [ (100 − NTA Percentile) ÷ 100 ] x 14,15,000 + 1

Subject-wise NTA Percentile — Why It Matters & Reference Data

NTA computes four percentile scores: three subject-wise (Physics, Chemistry, Maths) and one overall. Subject percentiles are used in tie-breaking and understanding them helps identify improvement areas.

Physics Percentile

  • 100 marks → ~99.10 percentile
  • 80 marks → ~97.20 percentile
  • 60 marks → ~93.00 percentile
  • 50 marks → ~88.50 percentile
  • 40 marks → ~80.00 percentile
  • 30 marks → ~68.00 percentile
  • * Used as 2nd tie-breaker after Maths

Chemistry Percentile

  • 100 marks → ~99.30 percentile
  • 80 marks → ~97.50 percentile
  • 60 marks → ~93.50 percentile
  • 50 marks → ~89.00 percentile
  • 40 marks → ~81.50 percentile
  • 30 marks → ~69.00 percentile
  • * Generally highest distributions

Maths Percentile

  • 100 marks → ~98.90 percentile
  • 80 marks → ~96.80 percentile
  • 60 marks → ~92.50 percentile
  • 50 marks → ~87.00 percentile
  • 40 marks → ~78.50 percentile
  • 30 marks → ~65.00 percentile
  • * Primary tie-breaker subject

Tie-Breaker Priority (Official NTA Order): (1) Higher Maths percentile → (2) Higher Physics percentile → (3) Higher Chemistry percentile → (4) Lower ratio of incorrect-to-correct attempts → (5) Older candidate age. Focus on Maths to maximize your tie-breaking advantage.
